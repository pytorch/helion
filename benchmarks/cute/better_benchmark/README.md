# better-benchmark: Inductor Triton vs Helion (CuTe and Triton backends)

[better-benchmark](https://github.com/eellison/better-benchmark) extracts the
fused regions TorchInductor compiles from ~160 real models (torchbench,
HuggingFace, timm, genai; inference and training) as standalone repros under
`repros/canonical/<repro_id>/`. This directory holds hand ports of a sample of
those repros to Helion and the tooling that compares them with the Triton
kernels Inductor generates for the same repros.

The better-benchmark checkout is found through `BETTER_BENCHMARK_ROOT`
(default: `../better-benchmark` next to this repository). The ports and
`selection.json` were made against better-benchmark commit
`824c970f25f052de6cf66a4c634df246af00bb1e` (`selection.json`'s
`better_benchmark_commit`): the ports read the repros' inputs by position,
and the scripts warn when the checkout is at another commit.

## Layout

- `kernels/<repro_id>.py`: one Helion port per repro (see `PORTING.md`).
- `selection.json`: the 48 sampled (repro, shape) points and why each was
  picked, then three extra ported repros (`"reason": "extra"`), generated by
  `select_kernels.py` from a better-benchmark all-shapes sweep
  (`scripts/bench_parallel.py repros/canonical --all-shapes --output ...`) and
  better-benchmark's B200 model accounting. Without `--shape`, the scripts
  use a repro's point from it (a repro outside it: its first shape point).
- `common.py`: loading repros, inputs, ports and pinned configs; numerics checks.
- `inductor_code.py`: dump the Triton code Inductor generates for a repro.
- `check_port.py`: check a port on both Helion backends (optionally with random
  configs) against eager PyTorch and the anchored numerics gate, a loosened
  variant of better-benchmark's (it also accepts a one-ulp difference per
  bf16 or fp16 element, and compares the outputs that depend on random
  numbers with Inductor's). Like better-benchmark's, its reference is eager
  PyTorch on float64 copies of the inputs with the graph's own casts kept,
  so for a graph that converts to fp32 after its loads it is fp32 math
  (`common.AnchoredGate`).
- `autotune.py`: cold full autotune of one port on one backend; records each
  kernel's winning `@helion.kernel(...)` decorator.
- `bench.py`: time Inductor (default and coordinate descent), the repro's
  hand-written Triton oracle, both Helion backends and a memcopy
  speed-of-light proxy, interleaved in one process, and check each on a
  CUDA graph replay, the way it is timed.
- `campaign.py`: run autotune and bench jobs for a selection across GPUs;
  a job during which another process computed on its GPU runs again
  (`--retry-contaminated`).
- `plan_run.py`: plan a campaign from earlier runs' records: every CuTe
  autotune, the Triton autotunes whose earlier records cannot be reused
  (contaminated, unsampled, or no longer generating the code they were
  tuned as), every bench, and the GPU-hours they should take.
- `report.py`: summarize a campaign: geomean, time-weighted and
  model-weighted speedups over Inductor with and without the T5 outlier,
  per-family rows, %SOL, Helion-Triton, and each kernel next to a
  `--baseline` run; contaminated and unsampled records are listed and left
  out of the aggregates.
- `audit_activity.py`: list a run's records whose activity samples do not
  cover their jobs (no samples, too many failed ones, a long stretch without
  one, or a sampling thread that died), with the numbers it went by.
- `ncu_profile.py`: profile one implementation of a repro under Nsight
  Compute (per-kernel duration, DRAM and L2 traffic, throughput, occupancy,
  launch geometry).

## Timers

`bench.py` records two latencies per implementation, both cold-L2:

- `bb_us`: better-benchmark's own methodology (single-call CUDA graph replay,
  `do_bench` minimum); includes one graph launch.
- `cold_us`: Helion's autotuner regime (`interleaved_bench`: L2 flushed before
  every call inside a batched CUDA graph, flush time subtracted); free of
  launch-latency quantization, so it separates small kernels better.

The rounds alternate the order of the implementations, and a reading depends
on what ran right before it (by up to a microsecond for a small kernel), so
each timing is the mean of its forward-order and reverse-order values over
an even number of rounds (6 by default). Records made before that took the
median of 5 cold rounds, a forward-order reading, and the minimum over all
bb rounds: `report.py --rebalance` recomputes their timings from the
recorded rounds.

Throughput is reported against better-benchmark's effective byte count
(`byte_accounting.count_bytes_effective`: unique inputs read plus outputs
written).

## How to reproduce

A run needs:

- A Helion checkout at the tree under test that nothing edits while the run
  lasts: the jobs import Helion from the checkout that holds `campaign.py`
  (run from a `git worktree` when you edit the compiler meanwhile).
- A better-benchmark checkout at `selection.json`'s `better_benchmark_commit`,
  found through `BETTER_BENCHMARK_ROOT` unless it sits at `../better-benchmark`
  next to the Helion checkout (next to a worktree it usually does not).
- GPUs from the machine's GPU board (`scripts/gpu_board.py`): `--board-gpus N`
  claims up to N free GPUs while jobs can start, renews the claims every third
  of `--board-ttl` and hands back GPUs no job can use. A renewal extends every
  claim of the board session, so run the campaign under a `GPU_BOARD_SESSION`
  of its own. (`--gpus` takes GPUs you hold yourself for the whole run, for
  example under `scripts/gpu_board.py run --gpus ... --ttl ...`.)

The study's numbers come from B200s; every record lists its GPU and the
torch, Triton, cutlass-dsl and Helion versions it ran with. `selection.json`
holds the 48 (repro, shape) points picked by model share and family, plus
three extra ported repros; `select_kernels.py`'s docstring has the command
that made it from better-benchmark's all-shapes Inductor sweep and B200 model
accounting.

A run in a new directory `RUN`, reusing what still holds of earlier runs
`PRIOR` (newest first) and compared with a baseline run `BASE`:

```bash
export BETTER_BENCHMARK_ROOT=/path/to/better-benchmark GPU_BOARD_SESSION=bb-RUN
S=benchmarks/cute/better_benchmark
# Plan: every CuTe autotune, the Triton autotunes whose earlier records do not
# hold, every bench. Checking a reusable record's pinned code needs a GPU of
# your own; --no-pin-check reuses nothing.
scripts/gpu_board.py run -n 1 --purpose "plan a better-benchmark run" -- \
    python $S/plan_run.py --run-dir RUN --prior PRIOR --provisional-triton --write
# Phase 1: the CuTe autotunes and the benches, which time Helion-Triton on the
# earlier runs' configs (flagged provisional in the report).
python $S/campaign.py --selection $S/selection.json --run-dir RUN \
    --backends cute --board-gpus 4
python $S/report.py RUN --baseline BASE
# Phase 2: the Triton autotunes, each point benched again after its own.
python $S/campaign.py --selection $S/selection.json --run-dir RUN \
    --backends triton --board-gpus 4
python $S/report.py RUN --baseline BASE
```

Without earlier runs one campaign does everything (`campaign.py --selection
$S/selection.json --run-dir RUN --board-gpus 4`, then `report.py RUN`). A
stopped campaign picks up where it stopped when started again with the same
arguments. Phase 2 keeps each phase-1 bench record as `bench/<point>.json.prev`,
so the two phases' Inductor and CuTe times check each other.
`plan_run.py --reuse-unsampled` also reuses Triton records made before the
activity sampling existed; `report.py` counts those ports only with
`--include-unsampled`, which counts every record made before the sampling.

The machine is shared, so the campaign guards the timings:

- A job starts only on a GPU that other accounts' processes keep below the
  5% that would contaminate it (below) and where no process of this account
  that the campaign did not start runs.
- Every job samples `nvidia-smi pmon` on its GPU once a second. A process
  outside the job that reaches 5% SM utilization (`common.CONTAMINATION_SM_PCT`)
  contaminates a bench record. An autotune record is contaminated once such
  processes have computed at that level for 1% of the job and at least 10 s
  (`common.AUTOTUNE_CONTAMINATION_SHARE`, `AUTOTUNE_CONTAMINATION_MIN_S`):
  a blip moves only a few of a long autotune's timings.
- The campaign runs a contaminated job again, and one whose activity
  samples do not cover it, up to `--retry-contaminated` times (default 2),
  on another GPU when one is free, keeping each such attempt as
  `<record>.attempt<k>` and its log as `<log>.attempt<k>`. The first clean,
  covered attempt stands; when
  every attempt is contaminated or not covered, the best does (a covered
  one first), flagged `contaminated: true`.
- `report.py` leaves contaminated records, and records whose activity
  samples do not cover their jobs (`common.activity_coverage_issue`), out of
  every aggregate (a bench record its kernel, an autotune record its port)
  and lists them. A port's timing counts only while its
  point's autotune record holds the kernels the bench pinned, so a phase-1
  timing on a provisional config stays out until the point is benched again.
